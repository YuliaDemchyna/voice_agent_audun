# Norwegian rules for an AI debt-collection call

*Researched 24 Nov 2026. Not legal advice. VERIFIED = primary text read; UNVERIFIED = secondary source or our inference.*

## Which law applies (read this first)
- The **1988 Debt Collection Act (inkassoloven, LOV-1988-05-13-26) is still in force**. A **new Debt Collection Act (LOV-2026-05-22-19)** has been passed but is marked *not in force*; the start date is set by the King (Kongen bestemmer). Finans Norge reports 1 Jan 2027 as the signalled date (UNVERIFIED). Design for the new act; it codifies most of Finanstilsynet's current guidance.
- The **EU AI Act is not yet part of Norwegian law** (Norway's AI act goes back out for consultation in autumn 2026 and is planned for the Storting in spring 2027). GDPR applies through personopplysningsloven.

## Summary: what the agent must do
1. Call only **08:00–21:00 on weekdays and 09:00–15:00 on Saturdays**, never on Sundays or public holidays. Make **no more than 1 call attempt a day, 5 a week and 15 a month** per debtor.
2. **Verify identity before saying anything about the debt.** If someone else answers, or it reaches voicemail, reveal nothing (duty of confidentiality, *taushetsplikt*).
3. Be polite, accurate, understandable and discreet. Use **no pressure, threats or misleading consequences**, and do not threaten to tell family or employers.
4. **If the debtor disputes the claim (innsigelse), stop collecting** and hand over to a human. Do not keep negotiating.
5. After an instalment plan is agreed, **send written confirmation** of the due dates and amounts. Plans with 2–3 instalments carry **no instalment fee** (*avdragssalær* applies only to more than 4 instalments).
6. Adapt to hardship or illness. Give guidance on payment difficulties, or point to **NAV debt advice (gjeldsrådgivning)**, when the debtor asks. Respect an **opt-out from phone contact**.
7. Say at the start that the call is recorded, why, and where to read more, and **offer a way to decline**. Disclose that the caller is an AI and offer a human (best practice now; the AI Act duty is not yet in force in Norway).

## Findings

**1. Good collection practice (god inkassoskikk), current act.** Collection must follow good collection practice. Methods that subject a person to unreasonable pressure, harm or inconvenience breach it. This also covers creditors collecting their own claims. *Source:* https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1988-05-13-26, § 8, paragraphs 1–2. **VERIFIED.** Consequence: if § 8 is breached, the debtor does not owe collection costs (§ 17, paragraph 4). **VERIFIED**, same URL.

**2. Good collection practice, new act.** Collection must weigh the interests of both creditor and debtor. The prohibited methods are those causing "undue pressure, harm or inconvenience", including unnecessary legal steps. Collection must be **adjusted when the debtor is known to be in a particularly difficult situation** (serious illness, institutional stay, payment problems). *Source:* https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2026-05-22-19, § 14, paragraphs 1–4. **VERIFIED** (not yet in force).

**3. Communication and contact hours, new act.** Communication must be polite, correct, understandable, discreet and adapted to the debtor (paragraph 1). Electronic and oral contact is allowed only on weekdays 08–21 and Saturdays 09–15, and never on public holidays (paragraph 2). The collection agency must be reachable by phone (paragraph 3). *Source:* same URL, § 15. **VERIFIED.**

**4. Phone collection today (Finanstilsynet circular on phone collection, *Telefonisk inkassoverksemd*, rundskriv 5/2022).**
- (a) The same hours as finding 3 apply. Contact outside them counts as a breach of good collection practice (section 3).
- (b) Consumers: at most 1 call attempt a day, 5 a week and 15 a month **per debtor across all their cases**. Calls the debtor asks for, or has agreed to, do not count. The total pressure from letters, email and SMS is also weighed (section 4).
- (c) Firms must check that a number belongs to the debtor and not to a relative or employer. They must not send SMS with case details unless they are sure the debtor receives it. Calling or texting the wrong person is a named risk (sections 2 and 5).
- (d) **Automated diallers** must enforce the hour and frequency limits. Firms need enough staff to answer when a debtor picks up. They must keep a **register of debtors who opted out of phone contact** and exclude those debtors from automated calls (section 5).
- (e) If a debtor says they do not want calls, firms should respect it as far as possible.

*Source:* https://finanstilsynet.no/nyhetsarkiv/rundskriv/2022/telefonisk-inkassoverksemd. **VERIFIED.** The circular says nothing specific about AI agents or voicemail. For voicemail we infer "leave no case details" from (c) and finding 5 (**UNVERIFIED** inference).

**5. Confidentiality and third parties (taushetsplikt).** The collector, its staff and its helpers must keep personal information confidential. *Current act:* https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1988-05-13-26, § 28, paragraph 1 (**VERIFIED**). *New act:* § 37, paragraphs 1–2, with narrow exceptions such as informing the creditor, credit reporting and Økokrim (**VERIFIED**). Finanstilsynet's consumer page says collectors may not threaten to tell family, friends or the employer about the debt: https://finanstilsynet.no/forbrukerinformasjon/inkassovirksomhet (**VERIFIED** guidance, not statute).

**6. Disputes (innsigelser).**
- *New act:* if an objection casts doubt on the claim, **collection must stop** while it is investigated (§ 24, paragraph 2). The agency must have fair, transparent procedures for objections and give a written, reasoned answer when the debtor is not fully upheld. A disputed claim goes to court if the creditor still wants to pursue it (§ 25, paragraphs 1 and 3). Debtor inquiries must be answered within one week, or with an interim reply plus a consideration of pausing collection (§ 17). *Source:* https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2026-05-22-19. **VERIFIED.**
- *Current practice:* Finanstilsynet says a disputed claim that is not obviously baseless cannot proceed by ordinary collection. The collector must reach an agreement, take it to the conciliation board (forliksrådet), or close the case (consumer page above, **VERIFIED** guidance). In the current act, costs are not recoverable when the debtor had objections that were reasonable to examine (§ 17, paragraph 2; **VERIFIED**).

**7. Instalment plans (avdragsordning / nedbetalingsordning).**
- A plan agreed by the collector **binds the creditor** (current act § 13; new act § 27). **VERIFIED.**
- *New act:* when a deferral or instalment plan is agreed, the debtor must get **written information about the new due dates and the amount for each payment** (§ 16, paragraph 4). **VERIFIED.**
- Fees: the maximum collection costs may be raised by 1.5 × the collection rate (*inkassosats*, NOK 750 in 2026) **only for plans with more than 4 instalments**. So the agent's 2–3 instalment plans carry no instalment fee. *Sources:* https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/1989-07-14-562, § 2-4 and § 1-1 (**VERIFIED**). Finanstilsynet circular 5/2013, "Hovudregel": https://finanstilsynet.no/contentassets/e8976759f39a4c979a8dc6ddd26f91de/rundskriv_5_2013.pdf (**VERIFIED**).
- The collection regulation (inkassoforskriften) itself contains no rules on contact, objections or good collection practice. Those rules are in the act and in Finanstilsynet's circulars (**VERIFIED**, same URL).

**8. Hardship and debt advice.** *New act:* when the debtor asks, the agency must give guidance on payment difficulties **or refer the debtor to bodies that can help** (§ 16, paragraph 3). Collection notices must say how to contact the collector about payment difficulties (§ 20, paragraph 3). **VERIFIED**; the act does not name NAV. Finanstilsynet points debtors to **NAV gjeldsrådgivning** (consumer page, **VERIFIED**). Recommended script: on any hardship signal, offer NAV's debt advice and a human handover.

**9. Recording calls (Datatilsynet).** Businesses must **as a rule ask for consent** before recording a customer call. Recording to **document an agreement requires consent**. Before the call, tell the person that it *is* recorded (not "may be"), why, and where to read more online. **Offer a way to decline**, for example a keypress. *Sources:* https://www.datatilsynet.no/regelverk-og-verktoy/sporsmal-svar/lydopptak/har-firmaer-rett-til-a-ta-opp-samtalen-nar-jeg-ringer-til-dem/ and https://www.datatilsynet.no/personvern-pa-ulike-omrader/overvaking-og-sporing/lydopptak/lydopptak-i-forskjellige-situasjoner/. **VERIFIED** (guidance; neither page covers debt collection specifically). Note: rundskriv 5/2022 also forbids letting the creditor listen in live or access recordings (**VERIFIED**).

**10. GDPR.**
- Data minimisation and confidentiality are required by Art. 5(1)(c) and 5(1)(f). Verifying identity before disclosure follows from these articles and from finding 5 (**UNVERIFIED** inference: no Norwegian source prescribes a specific verification method such as one-time codes).
- **Art. 22:** individuals may object to decisions based *solely* on automated processing with legal or similarly significant effects. If such a decision is necessary for a contract, the controller must at least offer **human intervention, a chance for the person to state their view, and a way to contest** (Art. 22(1)–(3), https://gdpr-info.eu/art-22-gdpr/, **VERIFIED** text).
- Whether an AI agreeing a pre-set, debtor-chosen plan is an Art. 22 "decision" is **UNVERIFIED**. A refusal of an out-of-policy request likely comes closer to one. Routing refusals and disputes to a human, and offering a human on request, is the safe design.

**11. EU AI Act transparency.** Art. 50(1) requires that people be told they are interacting with an AI system, unless that is obvious from context. Under Art. 50(5), this must be said clearly no later than the first interaction. *Source:* https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/ (**VERIFIED** text; applies in the EU from 2 Aug 2026). **Not yet binding in Norway**: https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/tung-vil-sende-ki-loven-med-endringer-pa-horing/id3169693/ (**VERIFIED**, 4 Aug 2026). Disclosing the AI anyway also supports the "accurate, not misleading" duty in finding 3 (**UNVERIFIED** inference).

**12. New act status.** Lovdata marks LOV-2026-05-22-19 as not in force, with commencement set by the King (§ 55): https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2026-05-22-19 (**VERIFIED**). The 1 Jan 2027 target comes from Finans Norge: https://www.finansnorge.no/contentassets/416d893bcb3b440f80f937fd8e8d8d45/hva-betyr-ny-inkassolov.pdf (**UNVERIFIED**, not opened).

**Not found:** any Finanstilsynet or Datatilsynet guidance specifically on AI voice agents in debt collection, or any rule prescribing the identity-verification method.
